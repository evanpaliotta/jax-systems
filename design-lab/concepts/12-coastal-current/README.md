# Coastal Current — concept 12

## Design stance
A short, visual-led front door to a real five-page site, rather than another compressed one-page brochure. New concept only; not the approved Jax brand or customer launch.

## Key choices
- Layout: large editorial headline beside an original sea-glass ribbon sculpture; two restrained sections below, then footer.
- Typography: Manrope and DM Sans; italic Georgia only for the moving-word accent. System fallback works without Google Fonts.
- Color: warm shell white, sea-glass green, deep teal and a small sunset-coral signal.
- Original visual: local SVG with layered glass strokes, highlights, an animated route and interactive before/next-step examples. No third-party source graphics, logos or case studies reused.
- Architecture: homepage, Ways we help (nine illustrative scenarios and industry filters), How it works, Pricing and a preview-only contact page. Shared CSS/JS, no runtime framework or build dependency.
- Motion: pause/resume, reduced-motion support and static gallery previews. The homepage is not scroll-jacked.
- Copy: use the existing problem-first ideas; move explanations and prices to their own pages. The headline “Keep the work moving.” is a concept-level proposal, not a replacement for the canonical business positioning.
- Boundaries: no calendar/CRM/backend, no information submitted or persisted, no invented client proof or performance figures. Care is optional and separately scoped. Existing price numbers preserved.

## Research connections
Clay: confident typography and large, purposeful visual surfaces, not a wall of generic service cards.[1]
Cohere: a short promise, restrained editorial hierarchy and clear navigation into deeper information.[2]
Treg: an interactive hero object that makes a connection visible, rather than explaining the whole product in paragraphs.[3]
These are visual and structure references, not evidence of conversion results. Source homepages and screenshots inspected November 10, 2026.

REA's README was reviewed; no installation was needed for public-page layout/DOM and screenshot inspection.[4] No model, paid generation or external skill was installed.

## Trade-offs
- Strong: first impression, brevity, recognizable visual identity and browsable detail.
- Weak: requires visitors to click to learn scope/pricing; the original vector sculpture is lighter than a photoreal WebGL scene.
- Review next: art direction, headline, page split and whether the hero explains enough. Production booking and any final brand decision remain unapproved.
